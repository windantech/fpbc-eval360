Chapter 1

Introduction & getting started

Eval360 runs in a web browser. There is nothing to install.

Eval360 holds Future Planning for the Child's logframe, indicators, activities, community registers, child protection cases, monitoring visits, budget and donor reports in one place. A figure recorded once in the place it belongs appears everywhere it is needed — in the indicator, in the target-group table, in the quarterly narrative and in the export — without anyone typing it a second time.

Signing in

  1. Open Eval360 in Chrome, Edge or Firefox on any computer, tablet or phone.
  2. Enter the username and password issued by the Executive Director.
  3. Your account carries one role. The menu shows only what that role needs, and some screens open read-only.
Your role decides what you see
The role selector in the top bar is a demonstration control — it lets you look at the system as each member of staff sees it. In the live system you simply sign in as yourself. Case records are the clearest example: only the Executive Director and the Designated Safeguarding Focal Person can open one.

The one rule worth knowing

You never type a number twice. Attendance is entered on the register. The target-group table adds it up. The indicator reads the table. The quarterly report reads the indicator. If a figure is wrong, it is wrong in one place and corrected in one place.

How the system knows where something has reached

Nothing is moved along by hand. An activity is planned until a register is filed against it, and then it is done. A report is due because the quarter has ended and the calendar says so. An action is overdue because its date has passed. A finding is open until someone applies or dismisses it.

Chapter 3

Journey 1 — Delivering an activity

ACT-030, dissemination of gathered entitlements by the GVH Child Rights Councils to the village councils, planned for 6 Oct 2026.

1
Find it in the workplan Programme Officer · Menu: Activities & Workplan

The workplan shows every activity by quarter with its code, fund, planned cost and the groups it is meant to reach. ACT-030 is one of 8 scheduled for the current quarter.

2
Open the activity Programme Officer · Menu: Activities → the activity

Every activity record names what it feeds — the logframe output, the indicator, the target-group row and the reports it will appear in. That strip is the whole argument of the system on one screen.

3
File the register in the field Facilitator · Menu: Field Forms

There are nine forms, each naming the reports it feeds. They work offline: fill one in where there is no signal and it syncs when there is.

Open the Activity Attendance Register on a phone, choose the activity, record attendance by sex and disability, and submit.

4
Watch what moves — · Menu: Target Groups

Submitting the register does four things at once. The activity closes as delivered. The target-group table gains the attendance. Indicator 1.3.1 — duty-bearers engaged in interface meetings — moves from 12 to 62. And the Community Score Card advances from step 3 to step 4, because the dissemination is step 4.

The variance rule
A row that reaches less than 80% of its quarterly plan must carry an explanation before the KNH report can be generated. In this quarter that is extension workers, who were reached 0 of 25 because the psychological first aid refresher was postponed. The same postponement appears as the only activity outside the ±15% cost variance threshold, and as an alert on the dashboard. One event, three screens.
Chapter 4

Journey 2 — A child protection case

46 cases have been recorded since April. This is how one of them moves, and who may see it.

Case records are the most restricted thing in the system. Everyone can see the counts; only the Executive Director and the Designated Safeguarding Focal Person can open an individual record, and every time they do it is written to the access log.

1
A case is reported Safeguarding Focal Person · Menu: Case Management

Sex, age band and GVH are recorded. No child’s name is ever entered. The record also holds who reported it — 12 of 46 came from community members who are not committee members, which is itself an indicator of whether the community trusts the system.

2
Assessed, referred, supported Safeguarding Focal Person · Menu: Case detail

The median time from report to first response is 3 days. 19 cases have been handled under the Child Care, Protection and Justice Act and 27 under the community by-laws. 18 children received psychosocial support, of whom 13 showed improved well-being at follow-up.

3
Concluding it moves the indicators — · Menu: Indicators

Changing a case to Concluded immediately moves SO2 (cases followed to completion), 2.1 and SO4. Cumulatively 21 of 46 cases (45.7%) have been concluded, against an end-of-project target of 70%.

4
What a role without access sees Field facilitator · Menu: Case Management

Switch to a facilitator and the same screen shows the aggregates and nothing else. The record list is replaced — not blurred. No case data is loaded into the page at all.

How cases appear in a donor report
Never as records. The quarterly report carries counts by category and sex, with the boys/girls split shown as “<3” wherever it falls below three, because category and sex together could identify a child in a community of this size. No case identifier and no village-level figure leaves the system.

Journey 3 — A monitoring visit

9 visits have been conducted this project year, producing 17 tracked actions.

1
Conduct the visit Any officer · Menu: Monitoring Visits & Actions

The visit report follows FPC’s own template exactly: background, an agenda table of activity, methodology and remarks, general observations, a table of specific issues and actions, and the objectives of the next visit.

2
Every issue becomes an action — · Menu: Action tracker

An issue without an owner and a date is a note, not an action. The board shows 2 overdue, 4 open and 11 closed. Overdue items offer Remind, which notifies the named owner.

3
By-laws monitoring Facilitator · Menu: By-laws Monitoring

FPC’s nine-question checklist, scored Yes = 1, Partly = 0.5, No = 0. Mwakasangira stands at 88.9% and Mwanjasi at 77.8% at the latest round. Submitting a checklist moves indicators 2.3 and 2.3.1 directly.

4
The score card closes the loop MEAL Officer · Menu: Community Score Card

3 of 5 actions agreed with service providers have been delivered — including the borehole at CBCC-MK-04, which the village council raised and then put to the District Council directly at the September joint visit.

The logframe and indicators

85 logframe rows and 48 indicators, in FPC’s own wording and FPC’s own levels.

Most systems have four logframe levels. FPC’s has five, because it carries a Use of Output row between the sub-objective and the output. Eval360 uses FPC’s labels throughout and shows the generic equivalent as a grey tag beside them, so nothing has to be renamed to fit the software.

The matrix view is FPC’s own four-column layout, for anyone who prefers to read it as a table.

Indicators MEAL Officer · Menu: Indicators

Each indicator shows its baseline, target, current value and whether it is ahead of or behind the straight-line expectation for this point in the project. 28 of 48 carry a value today; the rest are annual, or depend on activities later in the year.

Some indicators are computed live and cannot be typed at all — those driven by the case register, the by-laws checklist, the CBCC register, the councils and the score card. They change the moment the underlying record changes. The rest are recorded against a period on the indicator’s own screen.

Baselines
44 indicators have no baseline value. Progress against them can be stated as a level but not as a change, which will matter in the annual report rather than this one. The Logframe Quality Check offers to set a baseline of zero on the indicators that count something the project itself creates, and says plainly which ones still need a survey.

What generating does

  1. Collects the activity records, indicators and case aggregates for the period.
  2. Builds every quantitative table — the target-group table, the indicator matrix in the donor’s own column order, the case summary, the photo annex.
  3. Drafts the narrative from those same figures, and marks it as a draft.
  4. Shows what is missing as a red block in the place it belongs, rather than silently omitting it.

Every number in a drafted paragraph carries the query it came from. Turn on Show data sources and each one is underlined and names its source, down to calcCaseStats · 2026-Q2 · total.

Narrative can be edited in place and is then marked Edited rather than Draft; Regenerate throws the edit away and redrafts from current data. The document prints, downloads as a Word file, and the CSSF target-progress annex downloads as a CSV.

Chapter 13

Logframe quality check

A review of how the logframe measures, not of what the project does. Current score 72 out of 100.

Eval360 checks the logframe against 40 quality rules and lists what it finds, with a severity, an explanation and a suggested fix. The structure of FPC’s logframe is sound; these are points where the measurement could be tightened.

Six of the thirteen findings can be fixed by the system itself and say what they will do before you press. The other seven need a decision from FPC — aligning two targets, adding an activity, writing a measurable indicator — and say so rather than claiming to have acted.

Ignoring is not fixing
Dismissing a finding records the decision but does not change the score, because the weakness is still in the logframe. Only an applied fix moves it.

Ask Eval360

Answers computed from the project’s own records at the moment you ask.

A question box with seven suggested questions. The answers are not stored text — every figure is read from the system when the question is asked, so the assistant can never be out of step with the rest of the screens.

  • Which indicators are behind and why

What it is given
Counts and percentages only. No case record, name or village-level figure is ever sent to it, and it says so in the footer of every conversation. Asked something outside its scope, it declines rather than inventing an answer.
Chapter 15

Users, roles and configuration

8 people, five levels of access, and the three things only FPC can supply.

Case detail is restricted to two roles. Finance is read-only for the MEAL Officer, who reports on spend but does not post it. Field staff have the registers they fill in, and reporting read-only.

Configuration

Three things in this demonstration are deliberately not invented, because they are FPC’s to supply. All three are editable on the Configuration screen:

  1. The registry. 15 villages, 15 councils and 20 centres carry codes, not names. Type the names in, or paste a two-column list from a spreadsheet. A name takes effect on every screen, in every report and in every export at once.
  2. The reporting calendar. Due dates are derived from a rule per report — so many days after the quarter or year ends. Three rules are still marked as assumptions rather than facts from the grant agreements; correct the figure and tick the box as each is confirmed.
  3. The brand. Colours are tokens, so changing one recolours the charts and the generated documents too. A logo upload replaces the mark in the sidebar and appears on every document the Report Centre produces.

Safeguarding rules built into the system

  • No child’s name is held anywhere.
  • Case records carry sex, age band and GVH only.
  • A village is named on a case record only where five or more cases share it.
  • Counts below three are shown as “<3” wherever category and sex are crossed.
  • Every opening of a case record is written to the access log.
  • Donor reports carry no case identifier and no village-level case figure.
  • A photograph without a consent declaration is withheld from every generated report.
  • The AI assistant receives counts, never records.
